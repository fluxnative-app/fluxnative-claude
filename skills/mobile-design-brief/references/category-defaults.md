# Category defaults

What a model draws when it is told only the category, and ways out that still fit the product. Patterns only; no app is named. Use the row to critique the brief (Pass 2): if the brief matches the default column, move at least one of palette, lead or structure, and write down what moved.

"Believable data" is relative guidance. Set real figures from the app's market with `app-sample-content`.

| Category | Default generated look | Ways out that still fit | What must lead | What makes the data believable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finance | Dark ground, neon green or violet gradient card with a balance, a row of round action buttons, a stat grid | Light neutral ground with one sober accent; the balance as plain large type on the ground; spending drawn as a real series | The figure the person checks most (balance, safe-to-spend, or this month vs last) | Uneven amounts with cents; merchants from the market; a month that does not end on round totals; income on its real pay day |
| Fitness | Black ground, electric lime or orange, a ring, motivational slogan, stock athlete photo | Neutral ground; the workout as a timeline or set table; the person's own progress as the image | Today's session or the next set | Weights and reps that progress unevenly; a missed day; durations not on the half hour |
| Habits | Pastel cards, emoji per habit, streak flame, confetti | One grid of days with marks; text labels; one accent for "done" | Today's undone habits | A streak broken once; 3–7 habits, not 12; done times spread across the day |
| Commerce | Full-width promo carousel, grid of identical product cards, red sale badges, a floating cart | Editorial list with one featured item; product photos at one ratio; price as the second line, not a badge | The product or the reason to open today (an order arriving, a restock) | Prices with the market's endings; stock counts that vary; a few items sold out |
| Food ordering | Hero food photo, orange or red accent, category tile grid, promo banner | A list menu with the person's usual first; pick-up or delivery time as the lead | The usual order or the time it will be ready | Menu prices from the market; options that change the price; a queue or wait that varies |
| Booking | Calendar grid in a card, purple or teal accent, "Book now" on every row | Slot list grouped by time of day; unavailable slots shown, not hidden | The next free slot or the person's next booking | Some slots taken; no bookable past slots; durations that differ by service |
| Social | Feed of rounded cards, gradient avatars, emoji reactions, a centre "+" tab | Edge-to-edge posts on the ground; real names in the market; reactions as text counts | The newest item from people the person follows | Uneven counts (3, 17, 42), not round; some posts without photos; replies of different lengths |
| Learning | Cartoon mascot, bright multi-colour tiles, XP bar, trophy | One course path as a list; progress as a number and a thin bar; one accent for "continue" | Continue where you left off | Lesson lengths that differ; a lesson half done; scores that are not all perfect |
| Travel | Full-bleed destination photo, gradient overlay, white serif headline, "Explore" button | The trip itself as a dated itinerary; photo cropped to 3:2 with no overlay text | The next leg: time, place, booking reference | Times in the destination's time zone; one leg with a long wait; local currency |
| Health | Soft teal or mint, heart icon, a ring of daily goals, stock doctor photo | Plain readings as numbers with units; a trend line of real points; status colour only for out-of-range | The latest reading and its trend, or the next appointment | Readings that vary within a range; one out of range; dates relative to today |
| Recipes / content | Full-bleed food photo cards in a grid, serif headline, cream ground with terracotta, a heart icon on every card | A list or two-column grid of one photo ratio; title and time as plain text; the saved or in-progress recipe first | The recipe the person is cooking or saved, else the newest | Real ingredient quantities, cook times that differ, one recipe with a long title |

## How to use a row

1. Compare the brief's palette, lead and main-screen structure with the default column.
2. Pick one way out (or write your own that fits the product) and put it in the brief's "Moved from the category default" section.
3. Keep what the category needs: the "must lead" column is about the person's task, not taste.

No row fits: name the nearest, then write the default look yourself in one line and critique against it.

A product that is built on one of the default looks (a brand gradient, a mascot the company owns) keeps it. Say so in the brief.
